1. Decoding Confusing Naming Schemes in 2026

Both Intel and AMD rebranded their chips around 'AI' NPUs. On Intel, Core Ultra 200V (Lunar Lake) prioritizes MacBook-rivaling battery life with on-package RAM, while 200H targets creator/gaming laptops. On AMD, Ryzen AI 300 (Strix Point) uses Zen 5 cores and RDNA 3.5 graphics—watch out for older Ryzen 7020/7320U chips disguised with '7000' naming that actually use ancient Zen 2 cores!

2. Which Should You Buy for Your Workload?

  • Video Editors (Premiere / DaVinci): Intel Core Ultra with QuickSync hardware H.265/AV1 decoding scrubs 4K 10-bit timelines noticeably smoother on thin laptops.
  • Students & Light Gamers (No dGPU): AMD Radeon 780M/890M and Intel Arc Xe2 both run GTA V, Valorant, and Forza Horizon 5 cleanly at 1080p Low/Medium.
  • All-Day Battery Priority: Core Ultra 200V (e.g., Asus Zenbook / Yoga Slim) delivers true all-day charger-free college/travel endurance.

3. Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Do Copilot+ AI NPU TOPS matter right now?
Mostly for Windows Studio Effects (background blur/eye contact) on battery and local speech transcription. Most coding/creative AI still relies on GPU or cloud APIs.
❓ Is Intel Core Ultra 5 enough, or do I need Ultra 7?
For browsing, Office, coding, and light 1080p editing, Core Ultra 5 / Ryzen 5 gives 90% of the speed at ₹15,000–₹20,000 less.
❓ How do I avoid old AMD Zen 2/3 chips with confusing 7000-series names?
Check the 3rd digit in AMD's 4-digit SKU: '7535HS' (3 = Zen 3+), '8845HS' (4 = Zen 4). Avoid '7320U' or '7520U' (2 = old Zen 2) if buying above ₹32,000.
❓ Can Core Ultra 200V RAM be upgraded later?
No—Lunar Lake 200V integrates 16GB or 32GB LPDDR5X directly on the CPU package, so choose 32GB upfront if you run heavy VMs.
