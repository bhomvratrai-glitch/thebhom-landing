1. AMOLED vs POLED vs OLED: Marketing Names Decoded

Every AMOLED and POLED screen is technically an OLED (Organic Light Emitting Diode) panel where each sub-pixel generates its own light. Samsung calls its active-matrix panels AMOLED / Dynamic AMOLED, while LG Display and BOE panels built on flexible plastic substrates are often marketed by Motorola or Nothing as P-OLED—which is lighter and resists shatter shocks better than rigid glass-substrate AMOLED.

2. Why '4,500 Nits Peak Brightness' Doesn't Equal Outdoor Visibility

Brands advertise 3,000–4,500 nits peak brightness measured only on a tiny 1–2% white HDR window. What actually matters under bright Indian afternoon sunlight is HBM (High Brightness Mode) full-screen brightness. Look for at least 1,000–1,400 nits HBM so Google Maps and UPI QR codes stay legible outdoors.

3. PWM Dimming & Eye Strain Explained

Most OLED screens dim by rapidly switching pixels on and off (Pulse Width Modulation). Cheap panels using 240Hz–480Hz PWM can trigger headaches for sensitive eyes at night. Look for phones advertising 1920Hz–3840Hz high-frequency PWM dimming or DC dimming above 30% brightness—or choose a flicker-free 120Hz IPS LCD if you are severely PWM-sensitive.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Is AMOLED burn-in still a serious problem in 2026?
Rarely for normal users. Modern pixel-shifting algorithms and dynamic LTPO panels prevent static status-bar burn-in for 3+ years unless you run navigation apps at 100% brightness for 8 hours daily.
❓ Does LTPO save noticeable battery compared to standard 120Hz AMOLED?
Yes, especially when reading static text or using Always-On Display, because LTPO drops refresh rate down to 1Hz or 10Hz instead of staying locked at 60Hz/120Hz.
❓ Why do some budget AMOLED screens look grainy in dark mode?
Low-cost rigid AMOLED panels sometimes show 'black smear' or slight mura grain below 15% brightness in dark rooms. Flexible OLED/P-OLED panels usually calibrate grey uniformity much better.
❓ Is Gorilla Glass Victus enough without a screen guard?
Victus improves drop survival on concrete, but fine Indian pocket sand (quartz) still creates micro-scratches. Use a thin tempered glass or hydrogel film if scratches bother you.
