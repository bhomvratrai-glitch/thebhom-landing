1. Why Gallium Nitride Runs Cooler in Smaller Housings

Traditional silicon transistors lose energy as heat when switching high AC wall voltage down to 5V–20V DC. Gallium Nitride (GaN) has a wider semiconductor bandgap, switching at higher frequencies with lower resistance—cutting wasted heat and shrinking internal transformers by nearly half.

2. Understanding Multi-Port Power Splitting & PPS

  • 65W ≠ 65W on Every Port Simultaneously: Plugging a phone into Port C2 while your laptop charges on Port C1 usually splits output to 45W + 20W (and briefly disconnects for 1 second during PD renegotiation).
  • USB-PD PPS (Programmable Power Supply): Essential for Samsung Galaxy 25W/45W Super Fast Charging, Google Pixel, and Nothing phones—PPS dynamically adjusts voltage in 20mV steps so the phone stays cool.
  • BIS CRS R-Number: Never buy uncertified ₹699 '100W GaN' bricks without an official Indian BIS registration mark and grounded/surge-protected prongs.

3. Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Can a 65W GaN charger safely charge a 15W pair of earbuds or smartwatch?
Yes! USB-C Power Delivery negotiates voltage from 5V upward—the charger never forces 65W into small accessories.
❓ Will a 65W GaN charger trigger Xiaomi 67W/120W HyperCharge or OnePlus SuperVOOC?
Usually no—OnePlus/Realme/Vivo SuperVOOC and Xiaomi HyperCharge use proprietary high-amperage protocols, so generic PD GaN chargers will charge them safely at 18W–27W instead.
❓ Why does my 65W GaN charger still feel warm to the touch?
Warmth (45–55°C outer shell) during full 65W laptop loads is normal thermal dissipation because the surface area is compact—not a defect unless it smells or trips.
❓ Do I need a special cable for 100W USB-PD?
Yes. Standard 60W (3A) USB-C cables cap charging at 60W; you need an E-Marker chip 100W (5A) or 240W cable to unlock 65W–140W speeds.
